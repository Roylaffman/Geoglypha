Historical Overview

Bronze Age Superpower of Anatolia

The Hittite Empire was a major Bronze Age power centered in Anatolia (modern Turkey). Its heartland was the region of Central Anatolia, roughly bounded by the Kizilirmak River (the ancient Marassantiya), with the capital at Hattusa (near modern Bogazkale, Corum Province). Hittite civilization is known for its cuneiform archives, monumental architecture, rock sanctuaries, and the earliest known peace treaty — the Treaty of Kadesh between Hattusili III and Ramesses II (c. 1259 BCE).

At its peak under Suppiluliuma I (c. 1344–1322 BCE), the empire stretched from the Aegean coast to the Euphrates River, from the Black Sea to the Levant. The Hittites left behind one of the largest cuneiform archives of the ancient world — over 30,000 tablets at Hattusa alone — representing the oldest known corpus of Indo-European texts.

Chronological Summary

From Assyrian Colony to Neo-Hittite States

Old Assyrian Colony Period

1950–1700 BCE

Kultepe (Kanes/Nesa) as Assyrian trading colony (karum). 23,000+ cuneiform tablets. Earliest Hittite presence in Anatolia.

Old Kingdom

1650–1500 BCE

Hattusa becomes capital. Unification under Hattusili I. Early Hittite conquests into Syria. Royal fortress construction.

New Kingdom (Empire)

1400–1180 BCE

Peak of Hittite power. Battle of Kadesh vs Ramesses II. Treaty of Kadesh. Imperial bureaucracy and monumental building program.

Neo-Hittite Period

1180–700 BCE

Successor states in SE Anatolia and N Syria after empire collapse. Luwian inscriptions. Karatepe, Arslantepe, Tell Atchana.
